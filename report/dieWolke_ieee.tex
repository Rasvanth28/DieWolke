\begin{abstract}
Optical Sentinel-2 imagery is frequently obscured by clouds, while Sentinel-1 synthetic aperture
radar (SAR) is not. We present dieWolke, a SAR-conditioned Brownian-bridge diffusion model that
reconstructs all 13 Sentinel-2 bands, and test four proposed additions in a controlled setting:
band-group attention over visible, red-edge/NIR and SWIR bands (C1), an NDVI-consistency loss
(C2), a per-pixel Laplace uncertainty head (C3), and a separate cloud-mask stage instead of
end-to-end training (C5). Every model is trained on 2,434 patches of the spring subset of
SEN12MS-CR and evaluated on its full official spring test split (1,500 patches) with paired
bootstrap confidence intervals. With single-step inference, our model reaches 28.12\,dB PSNR on
real clouds, 2.1\,dB above a gradient-boosting gap-filling baseline and 0.5--0.7\,dB below the
published UnCRtainTS and GLF-CR checkpoints, which were trained on far more data. The separate
mask stage is the clearest result: it beats end-to-end training by 1.09\,dB PSNR and 0.078 SSIM,
and masks from our Stage-1 masker are as good as exact ones. The uncertainty head ranks errors
(Spearman 0.43--0.55), and keeping the 50\,\% most certain pixels lowers NDVI RMSE by 26\,\%.
The NDVI loss lowers NDVI error by about 3.5\,\% at a 0.16\,dB PSNR cost, and band-group attention
does not help at this data scale. The deficit to the published models comes from partly cloudy
patches; under near-total cloud all deep models are within 0.2\,dB.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Sentinel-2 provides 13-band multispectral imagery every few days, but cloud cover makes many
acquisitions unusable, most severely in the seasons and regions where crop monitoring matters
most. Sentinel-1 SAR images the same ground through clouds. SAR--optical fusion networks such as
DSen2-CR~\cite{meraner2020dsen2cr}, GLF-CR~\cite{xu2022glfcr} and UnCRtainTS~\cite{ebel2023uncrtaints}
exploit this on the SEN12MS-CR benchmark~\cite{ebel2021sen12mscr}, and recent generative models
cast the task as a diffusion bridge from the cloudy to the clear image~\cite{hu2025dbcr}.

Downstream users of reconstructed imagery mostly consume vegetation indices and need to know
which pixels to trust. This motivates the four additions we study on top of a SAR-conditioned
Brownian bridge:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1, band-group attention:} separate attention streams, stems and output heads for
the visible, red-edge/NIR and SWIR bands;
\item \textbf{C2, NDVI-consistency loss:} an L1 penalty on the NDVI implied by the reconstruction;
\item \textbf{C3, per-pixel uncertainty:} a Laplace scale head trained on a detached mean and
calibrated by temperature scaling;
\item \textbf{C5, separate mask stage:} a Stage-1 cloud/shadow masker versus end-to-end training
without any mask.
\end{itemize}
(C4, an Indian Kharif-season benchmark, is future work and is not evaluated here.)

Our contribution in this paper is a controlled evaluation of these components rather than a new
state of the art. All of our own models share one architecture, one training budget and one data
subset, so each ablation isolates one change. We report paired bootstrap confidence intervals
for every difference, break the results down by cloud cover and by scene, and run the
published UnCRtainTS and GLF-CR checkpoints through the same evaluation code. Negative results
(C1) are reported as such.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{SAR--optical cloud removal.} DSen2-CR~\cite{meraner2020dsen2cr} introduced a residual
network that fuses Sentinel-1 with cloudy Sentinel-2 and the SEN12MS-CR dataset of co-registered
S1, cloudy S2 and clear S2 triplets over four seasons~\cite{ebel2021sen12mscr}. GLF-CR~\cite{xu2022glfcr}
adds global--local SAR-guided fusion, and UnCRtainTS~\cite{ebel2023uncrtaints} combines attention
with aleatoric uncertainty, both for mono- and multi-temporal input.

\textbf{Generative cloud removal.} DB-CR~\cite{hu2025dbcr} models cloud removal as a diffusion
bridge between the cloudy and the clear image with attention-based SAR fusion and reports
single-step inference. Brownian-bridge diffusion for image-to-image translation was introduced
by BBDM~\cite{li2023bbdm}. CloudBreaker~\cite{ahmed2025cloudbreaker} applies conditional flow
matching from SAR alone to four optical bands and evaluates NDVI after reconstruction.

\textbf{Classical gap filling.} Gupta and Verma~\cite{gupta2026cloudfree} reconstruct cloudy
Sentinel-2 pixels with tree ensembles before crop classification. Our gradient-boosting
baseline~\cite{chen2016xgboost} follows this spirit but is not their method.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Data representation}
Each sample is a 13-band cloudy image $y$, a clear target $x_0$ of the same location at a nearby
date, and a two-channel Sentinel-1 image $s$ (VV, VH). Sentinel-2 digital numbers are clipped to
$[0, 10^4]$ and divided by $10^4$; VV and VH are clipped to $[-25, 0]$ and $[-32.5, 0]$\,dB and
scaled to $[0, 1]$, following SEN12MS-CR conventions. A binary cloud/shadow mask $m$ comes either
from the data (Section~\ref{sec:masks}) or from the Stage-1 masker.

\subsection{Brownian bridge}
We use a Brownian bridge between $x_0$ at $t = 0$ and $y$ at $t = 1$,
\begin{equation}
x_t = (1-t)\,x_0 + t\,y + \sigma\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,\epsilon,\quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I),
\end{equation}
with $\sigma = 0.5$. At $t = 1$ the state equals the cloudy image exactly, so sampling starts from
$y$ without noise. Given a prediction $\hat{x}_0$, the reverse step from $t$ to $s < t$ has mean
$\hat{x}_0 + \frac{s}{t}(x_t - \hat{x}_0)$ and variance $\sigma^2 s(t-s)/t$. With a single step,
the sampler returns the network's prediction at $t = 1$; all results in this paper use this
deterministic single-step regime. During training $t$ is uniform on $[10^{-3}, 1]$, and with
probability 0.25 it is set to exactly 1 so that the single-step case is trained directly.

\subsection{Network}
The denoiser is a U-Net with four resolution levels (48, 96, 192, 192 channels), two residual
blocks per level and a sinusoidal time embedding. Its input is $x_t$, the optical condition (the
cloudy image with masked pixels set to zero), the mask and $s$. A separate SAR encoder produces
features at every level, and attention blocks at 1/4 and 1/8 resolution and in the bottleneck
cross-attend from optical to SAR features. The output is a residual on the condition: clear
pixels are copied and the network fills what is masked. The training loss is L1 on all 13 bands
plus the C2 and C3 terms below.

\subsection{C1: band-group attention}
In the full model, the input stem, every attention block and the output head are split into three
band groups: visible (B01--B04), red-edge/NIR (B05--B08, B8A, B09) and SWIR (B10--B12). Each group
has its own projection, self-attention, SAR cross-attention and feed-forward layers; the streams
exchange a pooled summary through a gate initialised to identity and are merged by a
zero-initialised projection. Each group's bands are predicted by a group-specific head. The
ablation \textit{no\_bga} replaces all of this by all-band equivalents with a similar parameter
count.

\subsection{C2: NDVI-consistency loss}
We add $\lambda_{\mathrm{ndvi}} \lVert \mathrm{NDVI}(\hat{x}_0) - \mathrm{NDVI}(x_0) \rVert_1$
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{ndvi}} = 0.1$, where $\mathrm{NDVI} = (\mathrm{NIR} - \mathrm{Red}) /
(\mathrm{NIR} + \mathrm{Red})$. The gradient of this ratio grows as the inverse of the
denominator, and with a small epsilon it drove our first training runs to NaN. We therefore clamp
the predicted denominator at 0.1 and exclude pixels whose target denominator is below 0.1 (water,
shadow, no-data).

\subsection{C3: per-pixel uncertainty}
The head also outputs a per-band log-scale $\log b$, and the model is trained with the Laplace
negative log-likelihood $|x_0 - \mathrm{sg}(\hat{x}_0)|/b + \log b$ with weight 0.05, where
$\mathrm{sg}$ stops the gradient~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties}. Detaching the mean means the
head only explains the remaining error and does not change what the mean learns. After training,
one temperature per band is fitted on the validation split to maximise likelihood~\cite{guo2017calibration}.

\subsection{C5: cloud masks and the two-stage design}
\label{sec:masks}
For real cloudy images the mask is estimated: s2cloudless~\cite{zupanc2017s2cloudless}
probability above 0.4, dilated, plus a spectral-index shadow heuristic~\cite{zhai2018cloudshadow}
restricted to the neighbourhood of clouds. For synthetic training and evaluation pairs, a real
cloud from another patch is blended onto a clear image with soft edges and a displaced cast
shadow, which gives an exact mask. The Stage-1 masker is a four-level U-Net (base width 32)
trained for 15k steps on synthetic clouds with binary cross-entropy plus 0.5 Dice loss; it reaches
a validation IoU of 0.964. The \textit{end\_to\_end} variant receives the raw cloudy image and no
mask at all.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Data}
We use the spring subset of SEN12MS-CR (ROIs1158) with the official scene split. Training uses a
seeded random sample of 2,500 patches of the training scenes; after skipping pairs whose
``clear'' target has more than 5\,\% cloud, 2,434 training pairs remain. Validation uses 300
patches and test 1,500 patches from the five official spring test scenes, all at
$256 \times 256$ pixels.
\textbf{Spring-only training and testing is not comparable with results on all four seasons},
such as Table~I of DB-CR~\cite{hu2025dbcr}; we therefore do not compare against numbers reported in
other papers.

\subsection{Training}
All bridge models are trained with AdamW (learning rate $2 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$, 1,000 warm-up steps, cosine decay to 5\,\%), batch size 8, random $128 \times 128$
crops with flips and rotations, gradient clipping at 1.0, fp16 mixed precision and an exponential
moving average (0.999) of the weights, on NVIDIA T4 GPUs. The full model and the matched baseline
train for 40k steps; the ablations, a 20k-step copy of the full model (\textit{full\_20k}) and the
end-to-end variant train for 20k steps. Each configuration is trained once (one seed). The
checkpoint with the best validation PSNR is evaluated with its EMA weights.

\subsection{Compared methods}
\textit{Matched baseline:} the same bridge model with C1--C3 removed. \textit{UnCRtainTS} and
\textit{GLF-CR}: the authors' published checkpoints (UnCRtainTS \texttt{monotemporalL2}, trained
with an L2 loss and without uncertainty output; GLF-CR \texttt{CR\_net}), run unchanged through our
evaluation. Both were trained on all seasons of SEN12MS-CR. As a check of our input conventions,
UnCRtainTS scores 28.83\,dB / 0.882 SSIM / 8.00\textdegree{} SAM on our spring test split,
against 28.90 / 0.880 / 8.32 reported on the all-season test split~\cite{ebel2023uncrtaints}.
\textit{XGBoost:} per-patch gradient boosting from S1 and clear pixels to each S2
band~\cite{chen2016xgboost}, with a model pooled over the training split for the 837 test patches
that have too few clear pixels.

\subsection{Metrics}
Following the SEN12MS-CR literature, PSNR, SSIM, MAE, RMSE and SAM (degrees) are computed on all
13 bands with data range 1, per image, then averaged. We add NDVI RMSE and versions of PSNR and
NDVI RMSE restricted to masked (cloudy) pixels. Differences between methods are paired over test
images, with 95\,\% bootstrap confidence intervals. Uncertainty is assessed by negative
log-likelihood, calibration error, the Spearman correlation between predicted scale and absolute
error, and the area under the sparsification error curve (AUSE)~\cite{ilg2018uncertainty}.

\subsection{Where the gap comes from}
Fig.~\ref{fig:cloudfrac} splits the test set by estimated cloud fraction. Under near-total cloud
(fraction $\geq 0.9$, 718 patches, 48\,\% of the test set), where only SAR carries information,
the four deep models lie within 0.2\,dB of each other: the full model is level with the matched
baseline ($+0.06$\,dB [$-0.02$, $+0.14$]) and GLF-CR ($+0.12$ [$-0.02$, $+0.28$]) and 0.20\,dB
[0.06, 0.33] behind UnCRtainTS. On partly cloudy patches (782 patches) the published models lead
by 0.78--1.17\,dB, which points at the use of visible pixels and the date difference between the
cloudy and the clear acquisition, the parts that most likely benefit from more training data.
The per-scene ranking also varies: on scene 44 both of our models beat UnCRtainTS, and on the
cloudiest scene (140, mean cloud fraction 0.92) the full model beats both the matched baseline
and GLF-CR. Because the spring test split contains only five scenes and patches within a scene are
correlated, the per-image confidence intervals understate the uncertainty of the overall ranking.

\subsection{Ablations of C1--C3}
Table~\ref{tab:abl} removes one component at a time from \textit{full\_20k}. Every removal
\emph{raises} whole-image PSNR: by 0.36\,dB [0.29, 0.44] without band-group attention, 0.16
[0.11, 0.20] without the NDVI loss and 0.15 [0.09, 0.21] without the uncertainty head. The NDVI
loss is the only component that lowers its target metric: without it, whole-image NDVI RMSE rises
by 0.0053 [0.0038, 0.0067], about 3.5\,\%; inside clouds the effect is not significant. Band-group
attention does not lower the error of the red-edge, NIR or SWIR bands it was designed for: per-band
MAE of \textit{no\_bga} is equal or lower in B05--B12 (e.g.\ B07 0.0563 vs.\ 0.0587, B8A 0.0595
vs.\ 0.0619).

\subsection{C5: separate mask stage versus end-to-end}

\subsection{C3: uncertainty}
On real clouds the predicted scale correlates with the absolute error (Spearman 0.431, 0.440 after
calibration), and temperature scaling lowers the calibration error from 0.085 to 0.055
(Table~\ref{tab:unc}). Dropping the most uncertain pixels steadily lowers NDVI error, from 0.182 at
full coverage to 0.134 when the 50\,\% most certain pixels are kept, a 26\,\% reduction. On
synthetic clouds the raw calibration error is 0.037 and the Spearman correlation 0.55, but the
temperature fitted on real-cloud validation data worsens calibration to 0.060: calibration does
not transfer between cloud types. Neither the matched baseline nor the UnCRtainTS checkpoint used
here outputs uncertainty, so no uncertainty comparison is possible.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\textbf{Training data.} Our models see 2,434 spring patches, while the published checkpoints were
trained on the full four-season SEN12MS-CR training split. Their lead is concentrated on partly
cloudy patches, which require learning how to combine visible pixels with SAR across a date gap.
Whether this gap is due to data or to architecture cannot be decided from these runs; retraining
on a larger part of the spring training split, which has 24,378 patches in total, is under way.

\textbf{Band-group attention.} At this data scale C1 lowers PSNR and does not help the bands it
targets. The additional per-group parameters may need more data, or the grouping may simply not
be a useful inductive bias for this task; we do not claim C1 as a contribution on the basis of
these results.

\textbf{Statistical caveats.} Each configuration is trained with a single seed, so the confidence
intervals cover variation over test images, not over training runs; differences of a few tenths
of a dB between our own models may not survive another seed. The spring test split covers only
five scenes. Real-cloud masks are estimates (s2cloudless plus a shadow heuristic), and the
validation split ranked the ablations differently from the test split.

\textbf{Comparability.} All numbers are for the spring subset and are not comparable with
all-season results in the literature, including DB-CR~\cite{hu2025dbcr}.

\section{Conclusion}
In a controlled single-step setting on SEN12MS-CR spring, a SAR-conditioned Brownian bridge
reconstructs all 13 Sentinel-2 bands to within 0.5--0.7\,dB of published models trained on far
more data and more than 2\,dB above classical gap filling. A separate cloud-mask stage is clearly
better than end-to-end training, and a learned Stage-1 masker is as good as exact masks. A
detached Laplace uncertainty head gives a usable reliability signal that substantially lowers
NDVI error when uncertain pixels are filtered. The NDVI-consistency loss trades a small PSNR loss
for a small NDVI gain, and band-group attention is not supported at this scale. Next steps are
training on more of the spring training split, repeating key runs with additional seeds, and
building an Indian Kharif-season benchmark with downstream crop classification.
